full_abstract: >-
  The costs of Local Public Enterprises (LPEs) are utilized as crucial information for enhancing financial soundness, efficiency, and the quality of public services. To ensure the reliability of cost information and establish a productivity basis, local public enterprises conduct annual financial audits. However, the major accounting auditors for local public enterprises are concentrated near Seoul and Gyeonggi-do (the Metropolitan Area), which indicates the clustering of the auditor resource pool and the information accessibility of the audited entities. This study empirically analyzes the impact of geographical features between local public enterprises and their auditors on the cost behavior of local public enterprises. Using the financial statements of local public enterprises from 2017 to 2020 and conducting regression analysis, this study finds that local public enterprises, either directly or indirectly operated by municipalities, exhibit asymmetrical cost behavior. Furthermore, when both local public enterprises and the accounting auditor are located within the same metropolitan municipality, the accounting auditor moderates the degree of asymmetric cost behavior in local public enterprises. This study is significant in empirically demonstrating that geographical features from auditors can be a factor influencing the cost behavior of local public enterprises, which provides policy implications for the auditing of local public enterprises.
