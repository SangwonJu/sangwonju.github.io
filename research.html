My dissertation examines how generative AI is reshaping street-level accountability. Taking a behavioral approach, I use experimental and computational methods to study how citizens and public employees form judgments when algorithms mediate their encounters. Accountability at the street level rests on these judgments. If bureaucrats come to attribute decisions to the system rather than to themselves, whether willingly or not, their moral agency erodes, and the democratic accountability that depends on them weakens as well.

Beyond the dissertation, I examine a second intermediary in these encounters, organizational structure. From an institutional perspective, I study how structure conditions citizen–state interactions and how those interactions, in turn, shape policy outcomes.

A full list of my work is also available on Google Scholar.
